Safety, Attire & Health Protocols

Safeguarding wildlife wellness and guest security is governed by clear standard operating protocols developed alongside the Kenya Wildlife Service.

checkroom

Recommended Bush Attire

Neutral tones (khaki, olive, taupe, stone) blend seamlessly with savannah scrub and avoid attracting tsetse flies, which are drawn to dark blue and black.

  • • Sturdy closed walking boots with ankle support for walking safaris
  • • Layered fleece or lightweight windbreaker for brisk 06:00 open drives
  • • Wide-brimmed safari hat with chin retention strap
medical_services

Health & Malaria Advisory

Zarafa sits at 1,180m elevation. While our semi-arid climate reduces mosquito presence, Kenya is a designated malaria-risk zone.

  • • Consult your travel physician regarding prophylaxis (Malarone / Doxycycline)
  • • Emergency Flying Doctors medical air evacuation insurance included with all stays
  • • Comprehensive on-site first aid trauma station and certified medics
policy

Conduct in Vehicle & on Foot

Animals see safari vehicles as a single harmless silhouette. Abrupt standing, dangling arms, or excessive noise can shatter this perception.

  • • Never stand up when vehicles are stationary near big cats
  • • Camera flash is prohibited at all times on game drives
  • • Drones are strictly forbidden across Kenyan National Parks & Reserves
